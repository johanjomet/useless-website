The Philosophy of Shannon's Useless Machine

The origin of the "useless box" traces back to American mathematician and cryptographer Claude Shannon in 1952. Shannon built a physical wooden box with a single toggle switch; flipping the switch triggered a mechanical hand that emerged from the lid, flicked the switch back, and retreated back inside. Renowned science-fiction author Arthur C. Clarke observed: "There is something unspeakably sinister about a machine that does nothing—absolutely nothing—except turn itself off."
